## Context
Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Not all out-of-court statements are hearsay; the purpose for which the statement is offered is critical. Evidence is only hearsay if it is offered to prove the truth of what the statement asserts, not for other purposes such as showing the effect on the listener, the declarant's state of mind, or that the statement was made.

## Task Details
Decide whether the given statement is hearsay.

## Constraints
Apply the definition strictly:
- A statement is hearsay only if it is (1) an out-of-court statement and (2) offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted in the statement.
- If the statement is offered for another purpose (such as to show knowledge, notice, effect on the listener, or the fact that the statement was made), it is not hearsay.
- Conduct is not hearsay unless it is intended as an assertion.
- Nonverbal conduct is hearsay only if it is intended by the actor as an assertion.
- Physical evidence or reports that merely describe physical findings, without asserting a fact, are not hearsay. However, if a report or record contains statements asserting facts, those statements may be hearsay if offered for their truth.
- Statements made by a witness while testifying at the current trial or hearing are not hearsay.
