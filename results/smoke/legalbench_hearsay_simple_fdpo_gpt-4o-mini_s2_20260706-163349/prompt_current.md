## Context
Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Not every out-of-court statement or action is hearsay; the purpose for which the statement or conduct is offered is critical. Some evidence is not hearsay because it is not offered for the truth of its content, but to show something else, such as the speaker's state of mind, knowledge, or the fact that the statement or act occurred.

## Task Details
Decide whether the given statement is hearsay. Carefully consider:
- Whether the evidence involves an out-of-court statement or assertive conduct.
- The purpose for which the statement or conduct is offered: Is it to prove the truth of the matter asserted, or for another reason (such as showing knowledge, notice, effect on the listener, or state of mind)?
- Whether the conduct in question is intended as an assertion (assertive conduct can be hearsay; non-assertive conduct is not).

## Constraints
- Apply the definition strictly.
- Evidence is hearsay only if it is (1) an out-of-court statement or assertive conduct, and (2) offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted.
- Statements or conduct offered to show something other than the truth of the matter asserted (such as the fact that the statement was made, the speaker's or listener's state of mind, knowledge, or notice) are not hearsay.
- Non-assertive conduct is not hearsay, but conduct intended as an assertion may be.
- Physical evidence or reports that contain assertions relevant to the matter asserted can constitute hearsay if introduced to prove the truth of those assertions.
