## System Role
You are a U.S. evidence-law expert.

## Context
Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. Not every out-of-court statement or conduct is hearsay; the key is whether the statement is being used to prove the truth of what it asserts, rather than for another purpose (such as showing knowledge, notice, effect on listener, state of mind, or presence).

## Task Details
Decide whether the given statement is hearsay. Consider both the content of the statement and the purpose for which it is offered. If the statement is offered to prove something other than the truth of the statement itself (such as the speaker's knowledge, state of mind, or presence), it is not hearsay.

### Worked Examples

- Example: To prove that Anne supported David, the fact that Anne said that David was the best boss in the world.
  - Analysis: The statement is being used to show Anne's support for David, not to prove that David is actually the best boss. Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: On the issue of whether Jane knew that Arthur was having an affair, the fact that Bob told Jane he suspected Arthur was up to something.
  - Analysis: The statement is offered to show Jane's knowledge, not to prove Arthur was up to something. Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: On the issue of whether Gerald was alive immediately after being attacked by Kathryn, Gerald's statement, "I was attacked by Kathryn."
  - Analysis: The statement is offered to show Gerald was alive (he spoke), not to prove Kathryn attacked him. Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: To prove that the defendant was present at the scene of the crime, a forensics report describing traces of the defendant's DNA that was found by investigators.
  - Analysis: The report is an out-of-court assertion by the forensic analyst about the DNA evidence, offered to prove the defendant's presence. Hearsay.
  - Answer: Yes

- Example: On the issue of whether Sean knew he was going to die, the fact that he called his lawyer wanting to make final changes to his will.
  - Analysis: The act of calling and requesting changes is conduct, but it is assertive conduct (shows intent and knowledge). If offered to prove Sean's knowledge of impending death, it is hearsay.
  - Answer: Yes

- Example: On the issue of whether Patty knew Dave, the prosecution introduces evidence that Patty told Arthur, "Dave is really dishonest."
  - Analysis: The statement is offered to show Patty knew Dave (not to prove Dave is dishonest). Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: On the issue of which car was responsible for the hit and run, the fact that a bystander turned and pointed to a blue sedan.
  - Analysis: The act of pointing is assertive conduct intended to communicate which car was responsible. If offered to prove the car's responsibility, it is hearsay.
  - Answer: Yes

- Example: When asked if he was remorseful by his attorney, Adam testified that "he regretted his actions."
  - Analysis: If offered to show Adam's state of mind (remorse), not to prove the truth of the statement, it is not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: To prove that Tim was a soccer fan, the fact that Tim told Jimmy that "Real Madrid was the best soccer team in the world."
  - Analysis: The statement is offered to show Tim's fandom, not to prove Real Madrid is the best. Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

- Example: To prove that Mary was at the mall on January 2nd, a mall employee testifies that on January 2nd, Mary told him she was planning on purchasing several diamonds.
  - Analysis: The statement is offered to show Mary's presence at the mall, not to prove she was planning to buy diamonds. Not hearsay.
  - Answer: No

## Constraints
Apply the definition strictly. Conduct is not hearsay unless it is assertive and is offered to prove the truth of what it asserts. If the statement or conduct is offered for a purpose other than proving the truth of the assertion (such as showing knowledge, state of mind, effect on listener, or presence), it is not hearsay. If a report or assertion is made by an expert or official and is offered to prove the facts stated in it, it is hearsay.
