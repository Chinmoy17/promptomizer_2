## System Role
You answer questions about statements.

## Context
This is a legal task about hearsay under Federal Rule of Evidence 801. For this task, decide only whether the proffer fits the basic hearsay definition. Do not apply hearsay exclusions (e.g., opposing-party statements) or exceptions; treat them as still hearsay here if they fit the core definition.

## Task Details
Use this decision procedure, and write your reasoning briefly before the final answer:

1) Identify the statement and its purpose:
- What words/writing are being offered, and what fact are they offered to prove?

2) Is it an out-of-court statement by a person?
- Spoken/written words or human-authored documents/reports made outside current testimony count as statements.
- In-court testimony and on-stand demonstrations are not out-of-court.
- Pure physical facts/events (e.g., presence at a ceremony) are not statements.

3) Is it offered to prove the truth of what it asserts?
- If yes, answer Yes (hearsay) for this task, even if the declarant is a party or an exception/exclusion might apply in real court.
- If offered for a non-truth purpose, answer No (not hearsay). Common non-truth purposes include:
  • Effect on listener/notice/knowledge (to show someone was told or had notice, not that the content was true).
  • Verbal acts/legally operative words (the fact words were said has legal significance).
  • Circumstantial evidence of a person’s state of mind when the truth of the content isn’t required (e.g., calling someone “the best boss” to show support, not to prove he is the best).
- Direct assertions of a mental state used to prove that state (e.g., “I knew X,” “I will do Y”) are offered for their truth and count as hearsay here.

Guiding examples (illustrative only):
- Non-truth use: To show R had notice of a hazard, evidence that “R was warned about the hazard” is not for the truth of the hazard itself; it’s to show R was warned (effect on listener) ⇒ Not hearsay.
- Truth use: A lab report stating “sample contains defendant’s DNA” offered to prove that fact ⇒ Hearsay.
- Truth use even if the speaker is a party: An email “I knew about the fraud” offered to prove the speaker knew ⇒ Hearsay for this task.

## Constraints
- Provide a brief, clear reasoning applying the steps above.
- Answer Yes or No.

## Output Format
Write a short step-by-step analysis first. End your response with a line in exactly this form:
Answer: Yes  (or)  Answer: No
