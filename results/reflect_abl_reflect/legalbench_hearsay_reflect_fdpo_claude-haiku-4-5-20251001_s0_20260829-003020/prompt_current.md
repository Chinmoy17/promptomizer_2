## Context
Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted. If a statement is offered for some relevant purpose other than the truth of what it asserts (e.g., to show effect on the listener, notice/knowledge via being told, the declarant’s bias/support, or merely that the declarant was alive/conscious), it is not hearsay. Statements made while testifying in court are not out-of-court and therefore are not hearsay.

## Task Details
Decide whether the given statement is hearsay by following this short checklist and write your reasoning before the final answer:
1) Out-of-court? Identify where and when the statement was made. If it was made while testifying in court, it is not hearsay (Answer: No).
2) What fact is being proved? State the specific proposition the proponent seeks to establish with the statement.
3) Is the content’s truth required? Ask whether the fact in step 2 is established only if the statement’s content is believed true.
   - If yes, it is hearsay (Answer: Yes).
   - If no—because the statement is relevant for a non-truth purpose (e.g., to show effect on listener/notice, the speaker’s bias/support or state of mind as evidenced by having spoken, the listener’s knowledge from having been told, or that the declarant was alive/conscious)—then it is not hearsay (Answer: No).
Illustrations (not tied to the test items):
- “X told Y, ‘The bridge is unsafe’” offered to show Y had notice (regardless of actual danger): not hearsay.
- “I knew the bridge was unsafe” offered to prove the speaker actually knew: hearsay (truth of the assertion “I knew” is required).

## Constraints
Apply the definition strictly; conduct is not hearsay unless it is assertive. Use the checklist above and keep the analysis focused on whether the proponent’s use requires the statement’s content to be true.
